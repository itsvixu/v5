Hotels.com, 2018 to 2022

A credit card offer inside checkout

The Hotels.com Rewards Visa was offered to travellers in the middle of booking a hotel. I was the only designer on the card, and I redesigned the checkout module round by round.

My role
Senior Product Designer and the sole designer on the card. I built the card's landing page, the booking form placement, the checkout module and the application flow across app and web.
Partner
Wells Fargo issued the card on the Visa network. I set up weekly sessions with their stakeholders to design together and get sign-off.
What others did
Analysts ran the tests and wrote the readouts. I designed the tests and the experience.

The problem

Checkout is the most conversion-sensitive page on a travel site. A credit card offer placed there asks someone to consider a second financial decision while they are making the first.

Our own research said as much. People did not always notice the advert in the booking form, and one participant told us they would rather see it after booking, because applying in the middle was distracting.

Research I ran myself

I wrote the research briefs, built the prototype in Axure, ran the study on UserTesting.com with participants in the US and UK, and coded the results with my own tagging scheme.

People understood the card's features but wanted specifics. On mobile they could not find the APR. Most read "new" as meaning newly launched. When an application went to pending, it was not clear when they would hear back, and some felt uncomfortable.

I turned these into a prioritised list of simplifications.

Agree the acceptable cost first

The situation
With insurance added, fewer travellers scrolled to the bottom of the page, although the ones who did converted better.
Chose
We set an acceptable conversion threshold of −0.38% before the test ran.
Why
It meant the rollout decision was made on a rule we had agreed in advance, and not argued over after the result came in.

By the end I had written down what kept working.

  • A high-contrast colour combination that catches the eye.
  • A combination that had already won in earlier checkout tests.
  • A fixed place to signal limited-time offers.

Offers

In June 2022 I led the design of a statement credit offer. Approvals rose 5% in the month it launched.

On 30 June 2022 two changes went live together. A limited-time offer of three reward nights launched on the card's landing page, and the statement credit in checkout went up to $150. Account creation rose 145% and approvals rose 8% overall. The two changes were not isolated from each other, so I describe that as their combined effect.

The next step was "Show the Math", which works out the statement credit against the traveller's own booking. Before launch we expected it to bring about $400K a month in incremental member cash.

Changing the bank's side of it

The application itself was run by the bank. I benchmarked it against competitors and presented the comparison to Wells Fargo, with the cost of not improving it. They made changes on their side that simplified the experience for our travellers.

I partnered on the brief for an external audit of the cardholder experience. It benchmarked more than 140 functions against nine competitor travel cards and found 67 opportunities. I worked those down into thirteen prioritised items for the roadmap.

During a gap between product managers I took on some of the product manager's responsibilities through the beta and the launch. The experience was picked out by the Expedia Group CEO in an executive walkthrough.
