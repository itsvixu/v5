Expedia Group, 2022 to 2026

Leading a design team

I ran the design team for Expedia Group's credit card and loyalty programmes, and kept at least one problem on my own desk each quarter.

My role
Lead Experience Designer.
People
Three product designers reporting to me. Up to four contract designers at a time, flexed by project phase. A wider group of around fifteen product designers, content designers and researchers across four workstreams.
Where
US Pacific, US Eastern, the UK, Rome and India.
Above me
No Design Director in post for most of that period. I represented design at Loyalty leadership level alongside product, engineering and analytics.

The situation

The team was spread across five time zones. It went through repeated reorganisations and had a high rate of turnover, and a good share of the people were contractors. Setting the direction and the quality bar fell to me. The work also had to hold up under US financial regulation and our partners' legal and compliance review.

People

I set goals with each designer and gave feedback as the work went on, not once a year. I ran career conversations, and I wrote and argued the promotion cases that put people forward for the next level. I held contractors to the same quality bar as everyone else.

When I wanted to give someone room to grow, I did it on real work. I gave ownership of the accessibility audit to a designer on the team. At a three-day workshop in Rome I built in slots for team members to facilitate and present.

Earlier at Expedia I managed a contractor through a sustained performance issue, with daily check-ins, HR and the supplying agency.

"I've greatly benefited from our structured 1:1 mentorship sessions, which focus specifically on career growth beyond just project updates."

A designer on the team, 2025

Rhythm

I ran the weekly leads meeting, where design sat alongside product and engineering, and I joined the planning forum every three weeks. That put the design position in the room when decisions were made.

One way in for all the work

Chose
A Kanban model in Jira for intake and progress, linked to engineering's tickets. I set it up myself and got the product, engineering, content and research leads to agree to it.
Why
Work was arriving from four directions and nobody could see all of it.

Rituals that work in writing

Chose
A design review and critique process across the workstreams. Design jams and Miro boards people could add to in their own working day. Briefing videos and shared notes.
Why
A ritual that only works in a room does not work across five time zones.

Onboarding that survives turnover

Chose
About nine hours of recorded introductory sessions and deep dives, with background material in Confluence.
Result
New people became productive quickly through the reorganisations. The marketing team reused the material for their own onboarding.

A register of what we did not know

Chose
A shared list of the team's open questions about the credit card. Each one had a named owner, a note on why it mattered and where the answer would come from.
Why
Many answers already existed in analytics, in the card network's research or in earlier studies. The register sent people there first and kept new research for the questions that needed it.

Standards

With engineering I agreed a definition of done that included accessibility. Where there was urgency, accessibility could follow as a parallel track while engineers got started.

We agreed a working standard for handoff files. Changes were made on a branch and logged, and engineers were told before anything changed.

I wrote a design playbook for the credit card experience so new placements stayed consistent with existing ones. I directed when a workstream should build on a design system component and when it should propose a deviation.

After a pre-launch bug test logged more than 150 defects that leadership parked to protect a launch date, I made the case afterwards. On the next major programme, leadership agreed that design bugs would carry the same weight as functional bugs.

Hiring

I was actively involved in hiring for the London design team and worked with product and engineering on hires to the London campus. Over my career I have led or sat on the panel for more than 200 interviews, and I set the tasks, scorecards and methods used to review candidates.

At Trainline I wrote the brief for the in-house team and ran the design test. At Lloyds I was asked to hire and onboard the designers for the in-house squad.

What it produced

Team pulse ratings went up after I became workstream lead. In my final year, headcount across product and design was cut by 20%, and one promotion case still went through. When the team wanted to go to Config London, I pushed leadership to make it happen in a year when training and travel budgets were cut.

The team shipped the One Key credit card as native iOS and Android experiences alongside web. The "How it works" drawer designed for Save Your Way went into the Expedia Group Design System and was reused by other teams.

"In the absence of a Design Director he took on a number of additional responsibilities to ensure the design team had clarity and direction."
