Lloyds Banking Group, 2015 to 2016

Business loans and a mortgage tool for bank staff

I was the first in-house UX designer in Lloyds' commercial banking transformation team. I worked on a self-service loan application for small businesses, then on software for the staff who approve mortgages.

My role
User Experience Designer, embedded in the agency team as the bank's design representative.
Team
Six designers, working with a design director from the agency, with product owners, engineers and the bank's subject matter experts in the same London Bridge office.
Accountable to
The bank's transformation lead. I reported to Lloyds while sitting in the agency team.
A note on age
This is earlier work. I include it for the regulated lending and because it is my one internal tool for expert users.

On this page

  1. Business loans

A loan application that replaced eight manual processes

The first product was a self-service application for small and medium-sized businesses to borrow up to £1 million. Until then a loan passed through eight manual processes, each with its own team and much of it on paper, which slowed everything down.

The eight processes
Application intake and data entryKYC checks
Income and affordability assessmentCredit bureau review
Underwriting policyManagement escalations and approvals
Contract generation and completionManual release of funds

Prototype with real inputs

Chose
I built the prototypes in Axure.
Why
It could capture what a person typed and have the design react to it. A loan application changes with every answer, and a static prototype would have hidden the hard parts.
Tested
At Lloyds' own research lab in the Barbican, where we watched sessions through a two-way mirror.

One foot on each side

I was embedded in the agency team and reported to the bank. My job was to make sure that what the agency proposed would work in practice and stayed within Lloyds' design, identity and brand rules. Add one example of something you had to overrule.

Business owners were moving from a relationship manager to doing it themselves online. There could be no sense that the quality had dropped, and there was always a route back to a person.

Like every UK online banking product, it had to meet the AA accessibility standard and went through rigorous accessibility reviews.

When the agency's engagement ended I was asked to hire and onboard the designers for the in-house squad.

A mortgage approval tool for bank staff

After the loan work I moved to internal software for the staff who approve mortgages. The existing tool was old and cumbersome. The aim was to bring several online and offline processes together so that staff could get through their workload faster.

Watch the work where it happens

We went into the offices where the mortgage staff worked, sat next to them and watched how they did the job and where they got stuck.

Design inside the platform's rules

The constraint
The tool ran on Pega, which has specific rules about what can be changed.
Chose
To work directly with Pega's design engineers, learn the constraints and design around them.
What changed
Clearer read and write states, with labels. Colour used to make notifications stand out. Better tabbing across fields. Larger fields that are easier to read and check.
